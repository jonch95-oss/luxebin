Beauty labels are full of words that sound precise and often are not. Here is a calm guide to what the most common terms on makeup and skincare packaging actually mean.

"Clean"

There is no regulated definition of clean beauty. Each brand or retailer sets its own list of ingredients it chooses to avoid. Useful as a signal of a brand's priorities, but not a standard.

"Vegan" and "cruelty-free"

Vegan means the formula contains no animal-derived ingredients. Cruelty-free refers to testing: that the product and its ingredients are not tested on animals. A product can be one without the other.

"Fragrance-free" vs "unscented"

Fragrance-free means no fragrance has been added. Unscented can mean a masking ingredient has been used to neutralize a smell. If your skin is sensitive, fragrance-free is the term to look for.

"Non-comedogenic"

A claim that a product is formulated not to clog pores. Helpful for breakout-prone skin, though individual reactions still vary.

"Dermatologist-tested"

The product has been assessed under a dermatologist's supervision. It does not mean the product is recommended by dermatologists.

Reading the ingredient list

Ingredients are listed from highest to lowest concentration, down to about 1%, after which they can appear in any order. The first five usually make up most of the formula.
