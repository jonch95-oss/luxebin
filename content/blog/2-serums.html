Serums are where a routine gets personal, and also where it most often goes wrong. Layering two or three can work beautifully, as long as you follow a few principles.

Thin before thick

Watery, gel-like serums go on first; silkier, oil-based ones go last. Each layer should be able to reach the skin without having to push through something heavier.

Give each layer a moment

Wait until a serum has mostly absorbed before adding the next. Thirty seconds to a minute is usually enough, and it helps prevent pilling.

Hydration is the easy partner

Hyaluronic acid and other humectant serums sit happily alongside almost anything. If you are unsure where to start, a hydrating serum is the safest base layer.

Be careful with strong actives

Exfoliating acids and retinoids are better kept to separate routines, morning and night, or alternate evenings. If a combination stings or leaves skin red, scale back.

Less is often more

Two well-chosen serums usually outperform four. Introduce one new product at a time so you can tell what is working.

Finish with your moisturizer to lock everything in, and in the morning, sunscreen.
